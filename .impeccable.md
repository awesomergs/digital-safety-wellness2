## Design Context

### Users
College students learning to recognize scams, make deliberate privacy choices,
protect accounts, and find support after a digital incident. The course must
remain practical for a small student team to maintain and distribute.

### Brand Personality
Clear, reassuring, lightly playful. The user confirmed this direction before
implementation. Help students feel capable, not judged or frightened.

### Aesthetic Direction
A light learning workspace with a visible course path, readable lessons, and
hands-on activities that look like the situations being examined. Use restrained
motion, high-contrast controls, and meaningful feedback. Avoid a marketing hero,
decorative dashboards, nested cards, and novelty game mechanics.

### Design Principles
1. Put the next learning action and the student's place in the course in view.
2. Give lessons a readable measure and a consistent hierarchy.
3. Emphasize practice through real controls and explanatory feedback.
4. Preserve keyboard access, mobile reflow, reduced motion, and offline use.
5. Keep the single-file download, grading rules, and curriculum claims intact
   until the team supplies its research. Audit observations are not student
   research, and the password lab is a teaching heuristic, not a security test.
